Subject: High-Level Overview of Our Enrollment & Reconciliation Automation Work
Hi Curtis and Mital,
It was great to officially connect as part of the same team. Since we have had meetings together in different capacities before, I wanted to share a brief overview of three of the major automation and data initiatives we have built to support the Enrollment and Reconciliation processes.
Our overall focus has been to take processes that previously required significant manual effort and turn them into repeatable, traceable, and much more efficient automated workflows.
1. 834 Enrollment Data Pipeline
One of our major initiatives was automating the processing of inbound 834 enrollment files received from issuers.
Previously, obtaining and preparing this data involved manually navigating the SFTP/FileZilla structure, downloading large numbers of compressed XML files, extracting them, organizing them by issuer and date, and then preparing the data for analysis. Depending on the volume, this could require substantial manual effort across multiple days.
We automated that workflow end-to-end.
The pipeline now automatically discovers and downloads the issuer files, handles multiple compression formats, extracts and parses the XML data, standardizes the enrollment information, and loads the raw records into Azure SQL. It also maintains file- and run-level logging and prevents duplicate processing, making the process restartable and traceable.
As a result, thousands of files can be processed systematically in minutes or hours rather than requiring days of repetitive manual preparation, while giving the team a much larger and more reliable enrollment dataset for reconciliation and analysis.
2. RCNI Reconciliation Data Pipeline
We built a second data pipeline for RCNI / Monthly Reconciliation data, another area that previously required significant manual data collection and preparation.
The pipeline allows us to systematically collect, process, and load the reconciliation data into SQL so it can be analyzed at scale rather than working through individual reports/files manually.
We implemented this across both Stage and Production environments, creating a repeatable process for working with the reconciliation population and making the data much easier to query, compare, and use for downstream business analysis.
At scale, this has allowed us to work with tens of millions of reconciliation/discrepancy records while maintaining the underlying source-level detail needed for investigation.
3. Monthly Reconciliation UI Automation
In addition to the two data pipelines, we developed a Playwright-based UI automation framework for the Monthly Reconciliation workflow.
The automation navigates the reconciliation production environment, processes the issuer/month reporting workflow, captures the relevant reconciliation information, and consolidates the results into an HTML report.
Instead of manually navigating through each issuer and collecting the information individually, the automation performs the repetitive UI work and produces a consolidated output that we provide to Taylor for business reporting and analysis.
Together, these three initiatives have helped move a significant portion of our Enrollment and Reconciliation work from manual file handling and repetitive UI activity toward automated, reusable, and auditable processes.
The biggest benefit has not only been time savings. We now have more consistent data collection, repeatable processing, better traceability, and the ability to perform reconciliation and root-cause analysis across populations that would be very difficult to investigate manually.
I’m looking forward to continuing to build on these capabilities with the team.
